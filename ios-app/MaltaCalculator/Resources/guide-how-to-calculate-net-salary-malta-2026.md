# How to Calculate Net Salary in Malta 2026

Calculating your **net salary** in Malta means taking the gross pay you agreed with your employer and subtracting income tax, Social Security Contributions (SSC) and adding the government Cost of Living Adjustment (COLA) where applicable.

## The formula

```
Net = Gross − Income Tax − Employee SSC + COLA
```

Every payroll run in Malta follows the same steps — this guide walks through each one.

## 1. Start with annualised gross

If you are paid monthly, multiply the monthly gross by **12**. For weekly pay, multiply by **52**. This annual figure is the base for income tax brackets.

## 2. Apply the correct tax category

Malta taxes you based on your **tax status**:

- **Single** — default for unmarried residents
- **Married** — joint computation for married couples
- **Parent** — lone parents with qualifying children

Each category has its own set of brackets. Choosing the wrong one is the most common mistake.

## 3. Apply the 2026 income tax brackets

Use the bracket table that matches your category. Income tax is **cumulative** — you pay each bracket's rate only on the portion of income that falls within it.

> The Malta Calculator app uses the exact brackets published by CFR Malta for tax year 2026.

## 4. Subtract the weekly SSC cap

SSC is capped at **€559.31 per week** in 2026. Full-time employees under Category C contribute up to **€55.93 weekly** (new rate) or **€49.04** (pre-1979 rate).

## 5. Add COLA

COLA is paid in two flat amounts:

- **Q1/Q3:** €121.16
- **Q2/Q4:** €135.10

COLA is added to the net figure and is **not** taxed separately — it is absorbed into the gross for the relevant period.

## 6. Review the payslip

A compliant Malta payslip must show gross, tax, SSC, COLA and net on separate lines. If you spot a mismatch, raise it with HR — and verify with the Malta Calculator.
